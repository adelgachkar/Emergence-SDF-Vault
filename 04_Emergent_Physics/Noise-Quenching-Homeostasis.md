# Noise Quenching and Homeostatic Stability

## 1. The Stochastic Stability Paradox
A tetrahedral lattice coupled to an active, broken-time-reversal vacuum foam is subject to relentless thermal and stochastic shocks. A purely linear system would inevitably undergo parametric resonance and destructive blowup.

## 2. Dynamic Damping via Frustration Hysteresis
The angular deficit ($\delta \approx 7.36^\circ$) and its associated phase hysteresis ([[Hysteresis-Cost-TimeDelay]]) act as a non-linear dissipation reservoir:
$$\gamma_{\text{eff}}(\mathcal{A}) = \gamma_0 + \beta |\mathcal{A}|^2$$

1. **Low amplitude shocks:** The system behaves conservatively, storing energy in the internal dark modes ([[Bandgap-Phase-Recycling]]).
2. **High amplitude shocks:** Energy exceeds the linear bandgap threshold and is rapidly damped into internal lattice strain hysteresis, quenching destructive oscillations.

## 3. Structural Homeostasis
The lattice automatically locks into a robust thermodynamic attractor, maintaining coherent field propagation across macroscopic spatial extents without parameter fine-tuning.

Detailed analysis ([[Vacuum-Noise-Register-and-Ratchet]]) fixes the lawful role of the stochastic shocks: the vacuum noise is a **trigger and matrix element, never a motor** — the zero-point bath is passive, so it sets release *timing* (Kramers jitter ≈ 1.3% of the cycle in [[Phase-Debt-Oscillator]]) but contributes no directed work; the released energy is the accumulated phase debt itself. The quench of this note is the post-release return of that cycle. The coherent/incoherent crossover sits at ambient conditions: $\hbar\kappa \approx 23.8$ meV $\approx k_BT_{300\text{K}}$.
