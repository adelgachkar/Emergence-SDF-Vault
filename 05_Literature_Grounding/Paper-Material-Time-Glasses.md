# Literature Grounding: Material Time in Structural Glasses

## 1. Key Conceptual Anchors

* **The material-time postulate:** For aging glasses, relaxation proceeds identically when expressed in an internal clock $\zeta$ ("material time") rather than laboratory time $t$ — the clock advances only through structural rearrangements (Tool 1946; Narayanaswamy 1971; Moynihan et al. 1976; Struik 1978).
* **Modern predictive form:** Riechers, Roed, Mehri, Ingebrigtsen, Hecksher, Dyre & Niss (*Sci. Adv.* **8**, eabl9809 (2022), DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.abl9809) show the material-time formalism predicts nonlinear physical aging from equilibrium relaxation: $d\zeta = \lambda(t)\, dt$, where the clock rate $\lambda(t) \propto 1/t$ slows as rearrangements become rarer ("aging is one sentence, spoken ever more slowly").
* **Physical reading:** time dilation here is not relativistic — it reflects that each successive rearrangement is harder to achieve; the "second" of material time is the interval between configurational events.

## 2. Cross-Vault Mappings to SDF Framework

* [[Time-as-Residual-Rearrangement-Debt]]: SDF's central temporal postulate — "macroscopic coordinate time emerges as the residual accumulated debt of topological phase readjustment" — is a **cosmological generalization of the glass material-time clock**. The identification $d\zeta = \lambda\, dt \leftrightarrow d\tau_{\text{SDF}} \propto$ (phase readjustment events) is the direct translation.
* [[Hysteresis-Cost-TimeDelay]]: the delay bound $\tau_d \ge (1/\Delta\omega)\,\partial\theta_{\text{Berry}}/\partial\omega$ supplies the microscopic origin of the clock rate $\lambda^{-1}$ that the glass literature leaves phenomenological.
* [[Asymptotic-Tangential-MOND]]: if clock rates slow in weak-rearrangement (low-acceleration) regimes, kinematic time-dilation-like corrections to orbital dynamics follow — the same structural slot MOND occupies.
* [[Kinetic-Stability-and-Dispersion]]: the $1/t$ slowing of $\lambda$ is the stability mechanism: as rearrangements rarer, the lattice dynamically freezes into its current attractor.

## 3. Formal Bridge & Divergences

**Bridge:** both frameworks define time operationally as *accumulated configurational change*, giving SDF an experimentally grounded anchor rather than a speculative one. A concrete SDF corollary: $\lambda(t) \sim \tau_d^{-1}(t)$ with $\tau_d$ the hysteresis latency — merging the two formalisms in one line. **Divergence:** glasses are quenched, near-equilibrium systems on fixed laboratory backgrounds; SDF applies the clock to the substrate itself, where no external laboratory time exists. Establishing that the SDF clock is *relational* (defined by the lattice's own events, with $c_{\text{eff}} = \ell^*/\tau_d$ as its signal speed) is precisely where SDF must add physics beyond Struik's framework.
