## 1. Core Thesis & The Fallacy of Point-Particle Ontology

The foundational result of Laidlaw (2026) demonstrates that Bell’s theorem does not rule out local realism per se; rather, it excludes **point-particle local ontologies**. Standard derivations of Bell inequalities implicitly import a point-like ontology where:
1. Physical systems are localized at discrete spatial points.
2. Causal influences propagate strictly as retarded signals bounded by point-to-point light cones.
3. Hidden variables $\lambda$ are factorizable point-properties.

In a **continuous, non-linear field ontology**, physical states (beables) are intrinsically distributed over space. Consequently, field-field couplings across extended regions violate Bell's factorizability condition:
$$P(A, B | a, b, \lambda) \neq P(A | a, \lambda) P(B | b, \lambda)$$
This violation occurs **without superluminal signaling** and **without non-local action-at-a-distance**.

## 3. Retarded Action Paradoxes & Distributed Virtual States

Laidlaw demonstrates that standard retarded action between point sources suffers from three critical thermodynamic/kinematic flaws:
1. **Energy-Momentum Conservation in Omnidirectional Emission:** Reconciling spherical wavefront decay with localized absorption quanta requires artificial wave-packet collapses.
